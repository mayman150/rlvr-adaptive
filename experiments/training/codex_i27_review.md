1. **Finding 2 gives descriptive support, not equivalence.** I reproduced the RMS values. Common sampling seeds induce covariance that can differ between checkpoint pairs; they do not establish a sampling-noise floor. The 1.5-point window also allows profile drift, and reused checkpoints make the pairs dependent. ([drupalgsb-dev.cc.columbia.edu](https://drupalgsb-dev.cc.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/4261/glasserman_yao_guidelines.pdf)) Cheap test: align checkpoints to common pass@1 and compare paired excess squared distance,
   \[
   \Delta=\operatorname{mean}_{i,s,s'}[(\hat p_i^{M_s}-\hat p_i^{B_s})^2-(\hat p_i^{B_{s'}}-\hat p_i^{B_s})^2].
   \]
   Bootstrap whole problem vectors, keeping all checkpoints together; alternatively permute complete run labels within training-seed blocks if exchangeable. Report an interval against a chosen equivalence margin. Equal average distances can still hide systematic method-specific problem effects. ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4010955/), [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5502906/))

2. **Finding 4 is fair as a restrictive baseline check.** Its smoothing already uses a Beta(½,½) posterior mean. A better statistical check propagates \(p_i\mid c_i\sim\mathrm{Beta}(a+c_i,b+32-c_i)\) uncertainty through the shift and nonlinear pass@16 prediction, with \(a,b\) estimated from base counts. ([bayesrulesbook.com](https://www.bayesrulesbook.com/chapter-3), [mc-stan.org](https://mc-stan.org/docs/stan-users-guide/posterior-prediction.html)) My diagnostic recomputation reduces RMSE from **10.04 to 5.55 pp** when integrating the existing Jeffreys posterior—still poor, but materially different. The project’s frozen [frontier law](/Users/mmayman/Desktop/adaptive-rlvr/experiments/training/law.py:1), with \(\tau=-0.20\), gives **0.82 pp RMSE on the 65 T=1 checkpoints**. Compare per-problem residuals too. Success-logit shifts and token-temperature scaling are distinct hypotheses. ([huggingface.co](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/v4.25.1/main_classes/text_generation))

3. **The next test is useful for falsification, but cannot identify the mechanism.** Bracket overlapping pass@1 values, choose temperatures on validation problems, and compare held-out profiles and pass@16 with paired intervals. Increase sampling at the selected low temperature; monitor length/truncation—the base truncates **35.8%** of samples. At equal pass@1: matching curve **and** profiles supports a temperature surrogate; matching only the curve leaves problem-specific differences; falling below means tested temperatures provide less coverage than RL; lying above means temperature provides better coverage. No overlap is inconclusive. Even full agreement cannot establish that RL works through temperature-like sharpening.
