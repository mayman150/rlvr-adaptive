## 1. Diagnosis
Mostly right, with one correction. The setting is coverage-exhausted: at most ~6 points between RL pass@16 (87-89) and base pass@256 (94.9), on 511 problems with a 51-problem base-zero stratum — the 1-3 point method differences the literature reports are at this testbed's resolution floor. Early "frontier movement" is confounded with format (42.5% unboxed base; +18.4/-18.3 by step 25) and with the Qwen2.5-Math random-reward pathology (Spurious Rewards, arXiv 2506.10947: random rewards give +21.4 on MATH-500 for Qwen2.5-Math-7B; the effect does not transfer to Llama3/OLMo2 — verified).

Survives: (a) the speed-vs-coverage critique of fixed-step pass@k comparisons — methodological, setting-independent; (b) the within-setting null (RL-side interventions sit on the Dr. GRPO curve) as a claim about this regime; (c) warm starts transiently above the curve, decaying to ~+0.4 by step 150 — numbers verified.

Does not survive as stated: the abstract's thesis "coverage at a given accuracy is a property of the starting model." Two reasons. First, dose: 19,200 rollouts (~5/problem) cannot speak to ProRL (arXiv 2505.24864, thousands of steps, claims base-unsolvable problems become solvable) or BroRL (arXiv 2510.01180, hundreds of rollouts/example, claims to revive the ProRL plateau) — both verified claims we currently cannot contradict. Second, near ceiling, "every start decays to the reference" is exactly what saturation predicts, so "the frontier reasserts itself" and "the ceiling compresses everything" are observationally equivalent here. Also single-family exposure: the 26 public checkpoints are mostly Qwen-adjacent, exactly the family 2506.10947 warns about.

## 2. The claim
"At matched training compute (primary) and matched pass@1 (diagnostic), no RL-side intervention — outcome-diversity bonus, Pass@k Training (arXiv 2508.10751), BroRL-style wide rollouts (2510.01180) — raises pass@128 on a frozen hard set above the Dr. GRPO reference curve by a pre-registered Δ (2 points), on two model families; a small warm start does." Either half can fail independently; both directions are live.

## 4. Three weeks
Week 1: run Stage 0 as registered (~$230) with three amendments before launch: (i) dedup the Stage 1 training pool against both hard sets (blocking, above); (ii) Gate V must also pass on SmolLM3-3B, not only the 1.5B base — if only Qwen has headroom the setting fails; (iii) log distinct final answers per problem in the large-k re-eval (free diversity readout from the same samples). Week 2: equal-compute pilot, 2 models x 3 arms (Dr. GRPO; Pass@k Training; wide-group at identical total rollouts, group 64-128, fewer steps), 2,048 tokens, ~$1.2-1.5K; gate: hard_val pass@1 rises and pass@128 does not fall versus base, else re-mix difficulty strata. Week 3: warm-start arm from the Stage 0 harvest/PEER pools; one pre-registered hard_test read at pass@1-128; ~$1K buffer. Total within $3K only if capacity delays don't force idle re-queues — book one job per week, not daily.

## 5. Decision rule
User's intuition right: an RL-side arm lifts pass@128 at matched pass@1 by ≥Δ on both families, with gains concentrated on base-pass@128 = 0 problems — rollouts created coverage. Frontier thesis right: all RL arms sit on one curve; only warm starts rise above it and decay with training; base-zero problems stay zero. Pre-register the likely middle outcome as its own cell: wide rollouts lift pass@128 only where base coverage is nonzero — that supports a refined thesis (RL redistributes within support, per 2507.14843), and must not be spun as a win for either side.
