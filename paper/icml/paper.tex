\begin{abstract}
Comparisons of reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) at equal training steps mix learning speed with repeated-sampling coverage. We compare pass@16 at matched pass@1 with pre-registered decision rules and problem-bootstrap intervals. On Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and 7B, the interpretable comparisons of more than twenty interventions---advantage rules, replay, larger groups, rollout allocation, human-solution guidance, process credit, update-geometry changes and teacher distillation at the tested dose---are consistent with progress along Dr.\ GRPO's pass@16-versus-pass@1 curve within each setting, not with a change of the curve. Training $6.7\times$ longer raises pass@1 by $9$--$13$ points without meeting the registered coverage criterion. A frozen per-problem law computed from the base model's success counts predicts this curve. Evaluated under one fixed protocol, $26$ public checkpoints from other groups lie within $+0.9$ points of, or below, the law's prediction, including off-policy, external-supervision and random-reward recipes; the largest departures are downward and coincide with truncation. At matched pass@1, different methods show similar per-problem success profiles although their saved weight updates differ, and forcing a final answer recovers $16\%$ of Dr.\ GRPO's pass@1 gain on the base model. Starting-distribution changes remain a candidate lever, and we register a prospective test of whether the law forecasts their effect.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
RLVR trains a language model on automatically checked answers \citep{shao2024deepseekmath,guo2025r1}. A large literature modifies how sampled answers are weighted \citep{liu2025drgrpo,zhu2025nsr,walder2025pkpo}, which problems receive rollouts \citep{wang2026hora}, what is replayed \citep{zhan2025exgrpo}, or what external solutions are mixed in \citep{yan2025guidance,ma2025relift}. Many report higher pass@$k$ at a fixed number of steps. Yet RL-trained models are often beaten by their own base model at large $k$ \citep{yue2025limit}, and a method that progresses more slowly is less sharpened and therefore has higher pass@$k$ at any fixed step.

We separate speed from coverage by comparing methods at \emph{matched pass@1}: a method's pass@16 is compared with a reference curve interpolated at the same pass@1. All decision rules are registered before the corresponding runs, and intervals come from bootstrap draws over problems that recompute the reference in each draw. Our findings are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{One curve per setting.} The interpretable registered comparisons of more than twenty interventions lie on Dr.\ GRPO's curve within $\pm2$ points (\cref{sec:curve}). The pattern replicates at $1{,}024$ tokens and at 7B with $2{,}048$ tokens.
\item \textbf{Longer and stronger training does not leave it} (\cref{sec:long}). 1,000-step runs reach pass@1 up to $70.7$ while pass@256 changes by $-1.0$ to $+0.8$ points between steps 150 and 1,000.
\item \textbf{Public checkpoints fall on or below the curve predicted from the base model} (\cref{sec:public}). Across $26$ checkpoints and $38$ evaluations, the largest residual against a frozen law is $+0.9$ points.
\item \textbf{What is shared is behaviour, not a weight direction} (\cref{sec:mechanism}). Methods solve similar problems at similar rates; their saved updates overlap only as much as two seeds of one method; part of the gain is learning to finish an answer.
\item \textbf{Methods designed to move the curve did not} (\cref{sec:attempts}), including verified teacher solutions at the tested dose.
\end{enumerate}
\Cref{sec:discussion} discusses what can still move the frontier and the prospective tests now running.

\section{Setup and protocol}\label{sec:setup}
For problem $x$ with binary reward, $p_x$ is the policy's success probability. Over $N$ problems, $P_k=\frac1N\sum_x[1-(1-p_x)^k]$, estimated without bias from $n_x$ samples with $C_x$ successes as $\widehat P_k=\frac1N\sum_x[1-\binom{n_x-C_x}{k}/\binom{n_x}{k}]$ \citep{chen2021codex}.

\begin{proposition}[pass@$k$ at matched pass@1 measures spread]\label{prop:spread}
$P_1=\E_xp_x$ and $P_k=\E_xf_k(p_x)$ with $f_k(p)=1-(1-p)^k$ strictly concave for $k>1$; at fixed mean, a mean-preserving spread of $\{p_x\}$ lowers $P_k$.
\end{proposition}

\textbf{Training.} Dr.\ GRPO \citep{liu2025drgrpo}: advantage $A_i=r_i-\bar r$ within a group, a sequence-sum loss with a constant normalizer, one update per batch of fresh rollouts (near on-policy), no KL term, AdamW at $10^{-6}$, 16 problems $\times$ 8 rollouts per step, 150 steps, MATH training problems \citep{hendrycks2021math}. Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B \citep{yang2024qwen25math} is trained at 512 or 1,024 response tokens, Qwen2.5-Math-7B at 2,048 (FSDP, four GPUs per run).

\textbf{Evaluation.} 511 held-out MATH test problems with audited answer keys, 32 samples at $T=1$ (256 where stated), vLLM with per-problem sampling seeds and the training prompt; its agreement with the earlier Hugging Face evaluator was checked before use.

\textbf{Matched-accuracy comparison.} For a checkpoint with pass@1 $a$, the gain is $\widehat P_{16}-R(a)$, where $R$ is the reference's piecewise-linear pass@16 at pass@1 $a$. The Dr.\ GRPO reference for a seed is the larger of its training trajectory (base and saved steps) and its step-150 temperature sweep ($T\in\{1,1.15,1.3\}$). Gains average eligible checkpoints within a seed, then seeds with equal weights; 2,000 problem-bootstrap draws recompute the reference; draws in which more than 10\% of points are clamped make a contrast ``not interpreted''. \emph{Average equivalence} requires the pooled and each seed's 90\% interval within $\pm2$ points; \emph{moving the curve} requires a pooled gain of at least $+2$, a pooled 95\% interval above zero, positive estimates in every seed, and Holm correction where registered. Later registrations (20--22) also require each seed's 90\% interval within $\pm2$ for equivalence; clamping is assessed within each seed. Intervals condition on the trained seeds.

\textbf{A frozen per-problem law.} Each problem's log-odds $\theta_x$ start at an empirical-Bayes estimate from the base model's 32-sample counts and follow $\dot\theta_x=p_x(1-p_x)+\tau\frac1N\sum_{x'}p_{x'}(1-p_{x'})$ with $\tau=-0.20$, fitted once on Dr.\ GRPO's 1.5B checkpoints and frozen. A checkpoint is placed where the law's mean success equals its observed pass@1; pass@16 is then a prediction. The law describes the curve \emph{conditional on pass@1}; it does not predict training speed or endpoints.

\section{Longer and stronger training}\label{sec:long}
A natural objection is that 150 steps are too few to leave the base model's neighbourhood \citep{liu2025prorl}. We trained Dr.\ GRPO for 1,000 steps (about four passes over the training problems) at learning rates $10^{-6}$ (LH1) and $3\times10^{-6}$ (LH3), 1.5B at $1{,}024$ tokens, and evaluated with 256 samples at steps 150, 500 and 1,000. The registered primary reading asks whether pass@256 at step 1,000 exceeds the base's by at least one point with a Holm-adjusted 95\% interval above zero.

\section{Public checkpoints on the predicted curve}\label{sec:public}
Our curve could be specific to our recipe. We evaluated published RLVR checkpoints trained from the same base models by other groups, under one protocol (our prompt and tokenizer, $T=1$, 32 samples, pinned Hub revisions): 14 at 1.5B, evaluated at $1{,}024$ and $3{,}072$ tokens, and 14 at 7B at $2{,}048$. They span Dr.\ GRPO and GRPO recipes \citep{liu2025drgrpo}, SimpleRL, DAPO and RAFT++ variants, one-shot RLVR, rank-1 extrapolation \citep{wei2026relex}, replay \citep{zhan2025exgrpo}, off-policy guidance from stronger-model traces \citep{yan2025guidance}, interleaved supervised fine-tuning \citep{ma2025relift}, and correct- and random-reward reproductions \citep{shao2025spurious}. Of these 28 checkpoints (42 evaluations), two 1.5B checkpoints truncate more than 90\% of answers at $1{,}024$ tokens and fall outside the law's range at $3{,}072$; the remaining 26 checkpoints give 38 law comparisons. The reference is the frozen law computed from the same base model's counts at the same budget; at 3,072 tokens it is the only reference available.

\Cref{fig:public} shows the result. Residuals (pass@16 minus the law) range from $-7.6$ to $+0.3$ points at 7B, $-3.1$ to $+0.7$ at 1.5B and $3{,}072$ tokens, and $-4.6$ to $+0.9$ at 1.5B and $1{,}024$ tokens. The largest observed residual is $+0.89$ (a point estimate). Of the 38 law comparisons, 11 are equivalent, 22 inconclusive and 5 depart downward; no interpretable evaluation meets the registered upward-departure criterion. The checkpoints furthest below it truncate heavily at our budget: SimpleRL-Zero at 7B (28\% truncated), and four 1.5B checkpoints at $1{,}024$ tokens (30--52\%); three of them (ExGRPO, LUFFY, ReLIFT) reach residuals of $-1.57$ to $+0.72$ at $3{,}072$ tokens, while one-example RLVR remains at $-3.10$. Off-policy and external-supervision recipes are not exceptions: LUFFY at 7B $-1.0$, ExGRPO $-0.3$, ReLIFT $-1.7$, random reward $+0.1$, replay (RePO) $-0.1$ against $+0.3$ for its authors' own GRPO control. The registered per-checkpoint verdicts with Holm correction are reported in \cref{app:public}. With 2,000 bootstrap draws and Holm correction, no checkpoint departs upward under either reference; the downward departures are the truncation-heavy checkpoints above and the one-example RLVR checkpoint at 1.5B. Against our empirical 7B trajectory the largest gain is one-shot RLVR's $+1.8$ [$+0.95$, $+2.69$] (90\%, inconclusive); it lies where our 7B reference has no checkpoint between pass@1 30 and 62 and is linearly interpolated across the bend, and it is $+0.3$ against the law.

A fixed protocol is a limitation as well as a control: checkpoints trained with other prompts and longer budgets may perform better under their native settings. The test asks whether published RLVR recipes reach coverage beyond what the base model's counts predict under a common measurement; no interpretable evaluation met the registered upward-departure criterion.

\section{What is shared}\label{sec:mechanism}
\textbf{Per-problem profiles.} At matched pass@1 (within 1.5 points), the root-mean-square per-problem difference in success rate between two methods is $6.6$--$6.7$ points, the same as between two seeds of one method. A paired statistic---the excess squared per-problem distance of a method from Dr.\ GRPO over the distance between Dr.\ GRPO seeds---is $+2.1\%$ [$-1.9\%$, $+6.1\%$] of the seed-to-seed distance pooled over methods. Changes of the advantage rule leave a small signature (PSR $+7.5\%$ [$+2.6\%$, $+12.4\%$]); interventions that keep Dr.\ GRPO's advantages stay within seed variation. Mean success by base count is similar across methods at equal pass@1. This analysis is exploratory; per-problem outcomes still vary beyond what base success predicts \citep{yang2026kernel}, so the law is a conditional-mean description.

\textbf{Saved updates fail the shared-direction criterion.} Reported rank-1 structure of RLVR updates \citep{cai2025alpharl,wei2026relex} suggested that methods move along one direction. On saved 1.5B checkpoints, per-matrix rank-1 approximations capture $4.7$--$5.6\%$ of the update energy; the cosine between two methods' updates is $0.27$--$0.47$, against $0.34$ between Dr.\ GRPO seeds; PSR and NSR are the least similar pair ($0.27$). Removing each matrix's top singular component leaves $98\%$ of the pass@1 gain. Top singular directions remain aligned (0.82--0.91), and expected-gradient alignment is unresolved. (Checkpoints are saved in bf16, which distorts the rank-1 component; the remainder is preserved with cosine $0.975$.)

\textbf{Finishing versus accuracy.} At $1{,}024$ tokens the base model leaves $42.5\%$ of its answers without a final box (\cref{fig:finish}). Between the base and step 25, correct answers rise by $18.4$ points while unfinished answers fall by $18.3$ and boxed-wrong answers are unchanged. By descriptive accounting, boxing more answers at the base's accuracy among boxed answers corresponds to $+11.7$ of the $+36.2$-point three-seed mean step-150 gain; accuracy among boxed answers also rises ($37.6\%\to65.3\%$). As an intervention, appending ``Final answer: \textbackslash boxed\{'' to every unfinished base sample and decoding 32 greedy tokens raises pass@1 from $22.0$ to $27.7$, against $22.3$ for the same number of extra tokens without the cue: the cue-plus-extra-decoding intervention closes $16\%$ of the gap to Dr.\ GRPO's step-150 pass@1, at slightly higher compute; it is not an estimate of RL's causal finishing contribution. Across problems, post-RL success is negatively associated with the base model's boxed-wrong rate (descriptive). Temperature is not a substitute: lowering the base model's temperature to $0.4$--$0.8$ caps pass@1 near $29\%$ with $45$--$53\%$ truncation. Budget forcing at test time has prior art \citep{muennighoff2025s1}; our contribution is measuring how much of RLVR's gain it accounts for.

\section{Attempts to move the curve}\label{sec:attempts}
Each of the following was designed from a diagnosis and pre-registered with a selection or decision rule.

\textbf{More signal on rare problems.} Dr.\ GRPO's update on a problem is zero unless its group mixes correct and incorrect answers. Allocation by frontier value raises the share of success-bearing groups among pilot-zero problems from $18$--$21\%$ to $50$--$54\%$; at the two covered forecast coordinates (pass@1 37 and 40) the frozen law forecast $+4.1$ and the observed mean is $-0.05$ (the pooled registered estimate is $+0.14$). The tested allocation did not detect a registered frontier shift.

\textbf{External solutions.} Human-solution prefixes inside RL ($-0.05$), minimum-help process credit that credits failed prefixes by the smallest successful human hint ($-0.01$), do not move the curve; the warm-start advantage falls substantially with a larger evaluation budget but retains $+2.15$ points [$+1.10$, $+3.20$] (90\%).

\textbf{Update geometry and teacher signal} (one-seed validation pilots; selection required $+1.5$): removing 75\% of each update's component along the tracked dominant direction ($+0.20$ [$-0.84$, $+1.19$]); whitening problem weights by a sketched gradient Gram matrix ($-0.04$ [$-1.21$, $+1.05$]); adding the log-likelihood of verified 7B solutions on base-rare problems, 6.6 solutions per step ($+0.31$ [$-1.05$, $+1.35$]); and the same with the teacher gradient projected off the dominant direction ($+0.22$; $-0.03$ against plain teacher distillation). None qualifies. A gradient diagnostic with two independent rollout groups per problem found same-problem gradient cosines of $0.01$; the registered whitening reading was inconclusive.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
\citet{yue2025limit} show that base models beat RLVR models at large $k$ and that six RLVR algorithms perform similarly; we test that similarity at matched accuracy, across interventions and public checkpoints, and model it. The budgeted-search perspective \citep{sun2026boptr} maps RL operating points to base search budgets with $N_{\rm base}\approx\alpha N_{\rm RL}^\beta$. In a restricted single-decoder adaptation fitted on our Dr.\ GRPO checkpoints, with a 256-sample base, it predicts held-out pass@16 with RMSE $1.98$ points over 47 checkpoints, against $0.91$ for Dr.\ GRPO's curve. DARS \citep{yang2025depth} reports matched-accuracy plots; we add registered intervals. \citet{yang2026kernel} show per-problem heterogeneity beyond base success, which our aggregate result tolerates. Rank-1 and subnetwork structure \citep{cai2025alpharl,wei2026relex,mukherjee2025subnetworks} motivated our direction test. Reported departures include sharpness-aware updates \citep{kim2026flat}, which reach equal rounded mean pass@1 with higher pass@256 on AIME but not on MATH500 ($72.7$ vs $72.5$); BBG \citep{yuan2026overtraining}; base anchoring \citep{zhou2026pba}; ARMOR \citep{huang2026armor}; and EAPO \citep{lu2026eapo}, mostly at fixed steps. Distillation and mid-training change what RL can reach \citep{kim2025rlvsdistill,wang2025octothinker,zhang2025interplay,deshmukh2026projected}, and pre-RL pass@large-$k$ and held-out loss predict RL outcomes after SFT \citep{kang2025quagmires,malladi2026tailsft}. Reward noise as a change of clock has a theoretical predecessor \citep{rad2026rate}; training on atomic skills can solve compositions the base never solved \citep{wang2026primitives}.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
Within the tested regimes, RLVR methods differ in how fast they move along a curve, and that curve is predicted by the base model's per-problem success counts. Three explanations were tested: lack of on-policy signal on unsolved problems (allocation and teacher solutions at the tested dose did not help), shared skills that lift problems in the base model's order (consistent with the per-problem and finishing analyses), and too-short training (not supported for plain Dr.\ GRPO over four passes). Starting-distribution changes remain a candidate lever; whether the frozen law forecasts their effect is unresolved, and it already failed after one supervised warm start (RMSE $3.56$).

\textbf{Running, registered tests.} (i) Starting models fine-tuned on Qwen2.5-Math-72B-Instruct solutions for random, rarely solved and ``confidently wrong'' training problems at two doses, with the law's forecast of each start's post-RL curve frozen before RL, then RL from the starts with three seeds and a random-SFT control \pending{P30}. (ii) Teacher distillation at 7B with the 72B teacher \pending{P29}. (iii) Sharpness-aware RLVR on our curve \pending{P31}.

\section{Limitations}
Models are Qwen2.5-Math 1.5B and 7B (and OLMo-2 1B in earlier comparisons) on MATH-style problems with binary verification. Answer budgets of 512--3,072 tokens are part of the estimand; long chain-of-thought regimes, KL-regularized prolonged training, and other domains are not covered. Bootstrap intervals cover problem sampling conditional on the trained seeds. Average equivalence within $\pm2$ points permits smaller and checkpoint-specific differences. The law is a conditional-mean description fitted on Dr.\ GRPO; it fails after a supervised warm start (RMSE $3.56$) and in base-zero strata at 7B ($+7$ to $+23$ points). Public checkpoints are evaluated under our protocol rather than their native one. The per-problem, weight-space and finishing analyses are exploratory.

\section{Expected updates}\label{app:theory}
\begin{theorem}[Two moments of correctness-only rules]
Let a rule draw answers i.i.d.\ from $\pi(\cdot\mid x)$, stop at a time $N\le n_{\max}$ that depends only on the rewards observed so far, and assign advantages that depend only on the rewards. Then $g=\sum_{i\le N}A_i\nabla\log\pi(y_i)$ satisfies $\E g=c(p)\nabla p$ and $\E\|g\|^2=F_1D_1(p)+F_0D_0(p)+\|\nabla p\|^2X(p)$, where $F_r$ is the mean squared score norm of answers with reward $r$ and $c,D_1,D_0,X$ depend only on the rule and $p$.
\end{theorem}
Conditioning on the reward sequence fixes $N$ and the advantages, and $\E[\nabla\log\pi\mid r=1]=\nabla p/p$, $\E[\nabla\log\pi\mid r=0]=-\nabla p/(1-p)$. The expected update on each problem is collinear with $\nabla p_x$, but the aggregate update through shared parameters need not be; the invariance we observe is empirical, not implied. Under Dr.\ GRPO a group without both a correct and an incorrect answer contributes nothing, with probability $p^n+(1-p)^n$.
