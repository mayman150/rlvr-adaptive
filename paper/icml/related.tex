\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Advantage design and sample polarity.}
\citet{zhu2025nsr} report that negative sample reinforcement (NSR) preserves high-$k$ pass@$k$ better than positive sample reinforcement (PSR). Later work attributes exploration to negative samples \citep{tang2025polarity} or adapts the asymmetry and difficulty weighting of advantages \citep{ingle2026negative,yu2026symmetry,decugis2026fade}; like the original comparison, these evaluate at fixed training steps. We compare PSR and NSR at matched pass@1 on two model families, and show that trace-matched zero-mean noise added to PSR reproduces NSR's pooled early lag, which identifies estimator noise as a sufficient cause of the difference in progress rate.

\paragraph{The objective view of RLVR.}
\citet{davis2025objective} express binary-reward updates as ascent on monotone transforms of per-problem success. \citet{thrampoulidis2025shaping} interpret advantage shaping through surrogate rewards, and \citet{heckel2026weighting} characterize weights that minimize time to target accuracy. \citet{yu2025diagnostic} identifies pass@$k$ policy gradients as reweighted pass@1 gradients. For correctness-only advantages, our per-problem moment theorem gives $\mathbb{E}[g_x]=c(p_x)\nabla_\vartheta p_x$ and an exact second moment allowing $F_0\neq F_1$, the conditional mean squared score-function norms for incorrect and correct responses. Shared parameters permit prompt weights to rotate aggregate updates \citep{barakat2026interference}; a common accuracy--diversity frontier therefore remains an empirical hypothesis.

\paragraph{Diversity, pass@$k$, and evaluation.}
\citet{yue2025limit} report pass@$k$ crossovers between base and RLVR policies. CoT-pass@$k$ evaluates intermediate reasoning \citep{wen2025cotpassk}, while ``RLVR is a Kernel'' challenges deterministic prediction from base success and develops confidence bands for crossovers \citep{yang2026kernel}. BBG frames diversity collapse as overtraining and gates updates by posterior marginal pass@$k$ utility \citep{yuan2026overtraining}. DARS already plots pass@1--pass@$K$ trajectories and claims higher pass@128 at fixed pass@1, without intervals or a registered matching protocol \citep{yang2025depth}. Our matched-pass@1 protocol adds pre-registered criteria and bootstrap intervals that recompute an interpolated reference within shared pass@1 support. At matched pass@1, pass@$k$ summarizes the cross-problem distribution of success probabilities. Our frontier model predicts aggregate pass@16 from base success counts with a transfer coefficient frozen before tests across advantage rules, model families, and answer budgets; it fails the registered criterion after a supervised warm start.

\paragraph{Replay, off-policy guidance, and rollout allocation.}
TAR and EliteReplay replay diverse base-model solutions during RL \citep{li2026replay,moussa2026elitereplay}. LSPO scaffolds all-failure groups, LUFFY incorporates off-policy demonstrations, and ExGRPO prioritizes stored rollouts \citep{ding2026lspo,yan2025guidance,zhan2025exgrpo}. HORA maximizes posterior hit utility under a fixed budget with per-problem normalization \citep{wang2026hora}; Knapsack RL reallocates exploration budgets \citep{knapsack2025}. DARS adds rollouts to difficult problems, increasing its budget relative to its baseline \citep{yang2025depth}, and AR3PO combines allocation with response reuse \citep{zhang2025reuse}. MaxRL interpolates expected-reward and maximum-likelihood objectives as sampling compute grows \citep{tajwar2026maxrl}. We evaluate replay, base-seeded replay and larger groups at matched pass@1, and use a longer reference run where an intervention leaves the 150-step reference range.

\paragraph{Exploration bonuses and entropy.}
Unlikeliness reward upweights rare correct trajectories \citep{he2025unlikely}, while entropy-based controls regulate exploration through token-level updates \citep{cui2025entropy}. These interventions depend on trajectory likelihood or token entropy, extending beyond correctness-only advantage rules. We measure entropy separately on fixed text and policy-generated responses, and compare training checkpoints with a late-checkpoint temperature sweep. Higher temperature does not exceed the training frontier at matched pass@1 in our setting.
