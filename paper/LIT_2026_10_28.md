**2. Mechanisms that change the interpretation**

- **Sharpening has an inference-only analogue.** [*Reasoning with Sampling: Your Base Model Is Smarter Than You Think*](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14901v1) **P**, an [ICLR 2026 paper](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/13198), uses Metropolis–Hastings sampling from a sequence-level power distribution. Qwen2.5-Math-7B MATH500 accuracy rises **49.6→74.8**, versus **78.5** for GRPO. It spends additional inference compute. Your temperature sweeps therefore test a narrower family than “inference can reproduce RL sharpening.” 

- **Teacher dose and compatibility matter.** *RL vs Distillation* includes **30k verified QwQ-32B solutions** followed by RL; its distillation comparisons also change data, so they are not clean dose ablations. More directly, [*Projected Distillation*](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27467v1) **P** uses **four refreshed online passes** of localized teacher corrections. On a **hard MATH cohort**, Qwen3-8B with a Claude Opus 4.8 teacher reaches pass@1/pass@16 **5.6/35.4**, versus **2.0/20.5** for GRPO and **3.1/29.1** for direct-teacher SODA. Important limits: **one seed, best checkpoint selected separately per column**, and this cohort is not full MATH500. It establishes useful teacher transfer, not a matched-accuracy frontier move. 

- **Length and completion effects have substantial prior art.** [*s1: Simple Test-Time Scaling*](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19393v1) **P** fine-tunes Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct on **1,000 examples**. A training cap of **4,096** truncates **74% of traces**, versus **0% at 32,768**; AIME24 accuracy is **30 versus 50**. Inference-time “Wait” budget forcing raises **50→57**. Your contribution can be the measured completion component within RL, rather than discovering budget sensitivity. Describe your approximately one-third estimate as **endpoint accounting**: [INVESTIGATION_27](/Users/mmayman/Desktop/adaptive-rlvr/experiments/training/INVESTIGATION_27.md:65) does not identify its causal share, and [P28’s continuation intervention](/Users/mmayman/Desktop/adaptive-rlvr/experiments/training/PREREGISTRATION_28.md:66) estimates rescue effects under a changed inference procedure. 

- **Random-reward gains are model- and optimizer-dependent.** [*RLVR Can Be Spurious*](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10947v2) **P†** trains Qwen2.5-Math-7B on DeepScaleR for **300 steps, G=16, batch 64, KL=0**. Random rewards improve MATH500 **21.4 pp**, versus **29.1 pp** with correctness rewards; Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct does not reproduce the effect. Multiple updates plus clipping induce a biased mean random-reward update; the single-update/unclipped controls do not give consistent mean gains. Its greedy pass@1 and temperature-0.6 pass@k also use different decoding distributions. These results motivate separating reward information, optimizer bias, and judgeable-answer production when interpreting your format gains. 

- **Your “noise changes the clock” interpretation has a close theoretical predecessor.** [*Rate or Fate?*](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04411v1) **P** derives gradient scaling by **1−2ρ** under symmetric label noise in a simplified centered-bandit model; GRPO normalization and strong regularization complicate literal clock equivalence. In its Qwen2.5-3B CodeContests experiment—**1,410 steps, G=8, five seeds**—base/clean-reward/fully-random pass@1 are **12.8/20.8/13.4**. Preserve novelty in your empirical cross-method collapse, rather than claiming the general reward-noise clock idea.
