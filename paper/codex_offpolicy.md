1. **I agree with the distinction in motivation; I disagree with “at most changes speed.”**

   Exact importance sampling can preserve the expected gradient of the same reward objective. Practical clipping, masking, stale advantages and learning-rate schedules change the updates actually taken. The mismatch papers explicitly discuss these biases and optimization effects; identical reward objectives therefore do **not** imply identical accuracy–coverage trajectories. That invariance is your empirical finding, not a consequence of being “the same objective.” ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01826v1), [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09597), [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14286v1))

   There is even suggestive coverage evidence within your category (a): **ENTR, 2607.22186**, reports average AIME accuracy of **17.04 versus 17.54** for synchronous GRPO, with **pass@4 of 28.38 versus 27.68**. This is a small, unmatched comparison on general Qwen2.5-7B, without your uncertainty analysis; it is nevertheless enough to make “speed only” unsafe. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22186v5))

   Your category (b) is relevant, but separate **historical self-generated experience** from **external supervision**. Also, the categories overlap: Asymmetric REINFORCE’s LLM experiments use a delayed version of the same policy; TOPR’s generator experiments are self-taught. ARMOR anchors to an already RL-trained reference checkpoint, rather than the original pretrained base. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.20520), [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14286v1), [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.10481v2))
